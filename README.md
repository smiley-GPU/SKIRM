# LAST SEASON: modern tactical special operations (proof of concept)

A campaign of small special-operations missions, played as an **ops board**: each mission is a route of decision stages resolved with dice and visible odds.

**Base → Briefing → Insertion (with interlude) → Operation → Extraction (with interlude) → Debrief → Base**

**Play in the browser:** https://smiley-gpu.github.io/SKIRM/. The whole game is one file, `index.html`, plus operator portraits in `img/operators/`. It needs no server or build step, works on phones, and saves in browser storage. Open the file directly or serve the folder with any static server.

## What's in it

| Stage | Implemented |
|---|---|
| **Base** | The hub between missions. A **mission board** offers 2–3 operations in different locations, each with a weather forecast, threat and reward. Six departments upgrade with **Base Points (BP)** earned on missions: **Operations** (more missions), **Intelligence** (more intel, better forecasts), **Medical** (treatment beds that speed recovery and clear fatigue), **Engineering** (fabricate gear, kits and consumables, repair damaged vehicles), **Logistics** (more vehicles), **Barracks** (bigger roster, faster rest, trained recruits). Day-to-day spending is **Requisition (RP)**. |
| **Briefing** | Three steps under a pinned bar that shows the team, the transport cost and the RP left: **Intel**, **Team & gear**, **Transport**, then Launch (a greyed-out button always says what is missing). The chosen operation: location, time of day, the forecast and its reliability, what intel says about the enemy force, its officer and the target, and the route ahead. Up to six intel options (satellite, deep intel, weather recon, signals intercept, informant network, UAV overflight), some unlocked by Intelligence upgrades and some cheaper with the right specialist. Pick up to 4 operators (class, specialty, fatigue) with a weapon, armour, kit and utility from a limited **armory**; everyone also carries a **handgun**, so a primary weapon is optional (a handgun-only lead rolls one Assault die fewer outside close quarters, unless they are a CQB specialist), and transport in and out from up to seven vehicles (on foot, convoy, helicopter, light strike vehicle, boat, HALO jump, MRAP) that the location, the forecast and damage allow. |
| **Interlude (before)** | Each operator comments on the coming mission in the voice of their personality (20 personalities). The statement you keep becomes a one-mission effect. With a philosopher on the team you keep two, on different topics. |
| **Insertion** | Complications by vehicle, scaled by darkness and weather: hot LZ, hard landing, IED, ambush, a patrol boat, a scattered parachute landing. Vehicles can come back damaged. If the forecast was wrong and the weather grounds the ride, the team walks in. |
| **Operation** | A route of 5–6 stages: approach, perimeter, objective, optional target, exfil. At each stage pick an approach (Assault, Stealth, Recon, Tech), a lead and supporters, and gear to spend, then roll with the odds itemised. Success, success at a cost you choose, or failure. Push re-rolls the misses and spends **Nerve**, the team's grit, brought by rank (no Nerve, no push); or **burn** 2 Nerve for one sure success, once per roll (or a lieutenant's orders once a mission), and seeing a teammate knocked out or killed shakes the team: 1 Nerve gone; every 1 rolled brings a complication from the situation (a radio call, a dog bite, a jammed gate, a dropped magazine). A failure is taken first, so its alarm, return fire and wounds show on their own; then the **ways past the stage** open: a detour to the other entry, a breaching charge, a specialist doing what they do best (each of the ten specialties has one, said in their personality's voice), or forcing it; each costs time, fatigue, wounds or gear, and lost gear is gone for good. Approaches the team has no chance at are marked (or marked as possible only with the gear it carries), already on the entry-point choice; when nothing at a stage can work, the desperate measures open without a failure. Until something really happens at a stage, **Back** returns to the ways in or the route; gear picked for a test shows as a chip by the odds and is cancelled with a tap. Modifiers read from the player's side: red dots for what makes a test harder, green +1 for what helps. Critical events (QRF, time out, abort, KIA) open a stamped situation report; major ones show as banners. Two clocks: time and alert. A stage cleared with the last of the time still counts: the rest is abandoned and the team makes one hard roll at the exfil with no clock; run out on a failure and a way past is still open, or let the window close and walk out. At full alert a **quick reaction force** is called: two stages to finish the job, every test 2 harder and every failure a firefight, or run for the exfil now; with them on the team's heels the exfil is 1 harder. Once the area is alerted, bad hits turn critical sooner. The enemy gets more experienced from mission 2, every three missions, up to +3. Operators comment out loud on what happens (a success, a kill, a wound, a fall, the alarm, a new stage) in their personality's voice: speech bubbles that pop up and fade (green for good news, red for bad, yellow for remarks on the situation), kept in the log. |
| **Injuries** | No HP. Operators carry named injuries ("Broken finger: Assault −1, Tech −1"). Minor injuries pile up until a hit turns serious; a critical injury stops an operator leading and knocks them out unless treated; the unconscious die unless treated. Medkits and medics treat. |
| **Enemies** | A faction (militia, regulars or contractors) sets how well they shoot back and what you can take from them. A named **officer** with a trait (sharpshooter, tactician, brute, commander) may hold a stage and carries better gear. Take their position by force and they may die, go out with a grenade that wrecks some of your kit, escape (to a later stage with a grudge, or out of the mission to resurface promoted on a later one), or surrender: prisoners reveal the enemy force on every operation and, after interrogation, can be handed over or recruited. The HVT can be a financier, a bomb-maker or a **warlord**, the real enemy, who is harder to reach and gets back up once. |
| **Weather** | Seven location types, each with its own weather, encounters and routes. Climate hazards: heat waves, wildfire smoke, thunderstorms, flash floods, mudslides and dust storms change the odds, tire the team, and ground or block vehicles. Forecasts can be wrong, and the weather can turn mid-mission. |
| **Operators** | Class gives base dice, gear adds dice, and every operator has a **specialty** (marksman, demolitions, CQB, infiltrator, mountain warfare, combat diver, signals, linguist, pathfinder, endurance) that unlocks special approaches or bends a rule. **Fatigue** builds with leading tests, failures, long routes, heat and carrying the wounded; tired leads roll fewer dice, and resting costs time. **Rank** adds dice, Nerve and perks: a sergeant leading has a supporter step in front of the first hit, a lieutenant gives one free push a mission; a better barracks hires Privates First Class and Corporals. Weapons differ in more than dice (the AK is reliable in mud and dust, the LMG suppresses return fire, the carbine is light), Engineering level 3 adds night vision, ceramic plate and suppressed and anti-materiel rifles, and specialist tools (rebreather, climbing kit, encrypted radio, flashbang, smoke) lower the price of the ways out. Every stage opens with a line of scene-setting and every roll ends with one, built from the place, the weather, the lead and their gear. |
| **Personalities** | Twenty personalities, each with a voice and one small **quirk** that bends a rule in their favour, shown beside the personality and spoken when it fires: the stoic keeps the team's Nerve when someone falls, the joker makes rests count for more, the fatalist turns an accepted failure into Nerve, the rookie learns fast and at their first promotion becomes who their first missions made them (avenger, fatalist, glory hound, guardian or old hand), the gearhead never loses kit to water or a fumble, the glory hound rolls more at the objective and the homesick at the exfil, the guardian carries the wounded without slowing the stealth, the superstitious read the weather right, the mercenary scavenges spent gear, the planner has a cheaper way past once a mission, the lone wolf is better alone, the adrenaline junkie rides a hot streak, the philosopher keeps a second pre-mission statement, the avenger has a grudge against officers, the perfectionist turns one costly success clean, the optimist shrugs off the first failure, the old hand mentors privates, the true believer brings Nerve whatever their rank, and the quiet one makes no noise. |
| **Extraction** | Hot extraction if the area is alerted. Operators left behind try to get out on foot, and some become MIA. |
| **Interlude (after)** | Survivors reflect on what actually happened. The statement you keep becomes permanent: a trait, a bond between two operators, or a signature weapon. |
| **Debrief** | An after-action report filed from what actually happened, and the fallen with a teammate's words (their names go on a **memorial wall** at base). Injuries and recovery time, KIA/MIA, XP and promotions, gear lost and gained (faction loot, the officer's kit), RP and BP, replacement recruits. Every mission is filed in the **archive** at base, next to the memorial wall: the briefing as it stood (conditions, enemy, intel, transport, the team and their gear) and the after-action report. Then back to base. |

Every screen has a **?** button (how to play, how a stage works, and a glossary of RP, BP, alert, fatigue and the rest) and a **⋯** menu (sound and vibration, new campaign; restarting asks twice).

Art direction is a classified dossier: tactical maps (terrain, water and roads per region, with every stage placed where its situation belongs and intel-dependent hostile symbols), weather, insignia, patches, emblems, stamps and redaction are drawn in code. Pictures (location banners, key art, hostile portraits, department vignettes) drop into `img/art/` and show as labelled placeholders until then; `docs/art-guide.md` lists every slot with its size and prompt.

Design notes and balance numbers: `docs/abstract-mission-design.md`.

## Code layout

`index.html` holds styles, rules and UI in one script; the story text it tells lives in `data/`:

- **Rules data** at the top: skills, items, vehicles, intel, routes, situations, locations, weather, factions, officers, targets, specialties, base departments, injuries, classes, personalities and their lines. Every balance number lives in these tables.
- **Engine**: the base and mission board, mission generation, the operation rules, injuries, fatigue, weather, interludes and phases. Every command validates before it changes anything and throws `Rule` with a player-facing message on a bad input.
- **UI**: one render function per phase, re-rendered from the save state after each command.
- **Deterministic**: all randomness comes from a seeded generator whose state is in the save, so a reload continues the same game.
- **Story text** in `data/`, loaded before the game script: `data/voices.js` (what operators say, in each personality's voice: barks, interlude statements, specialist lines, words over the fallen, comments at base) and `data/story.js` (transit scenes, scene-setting, roll results, after-action wording). Text only; the rules never read it to decide anything.
- **Saves** are version 5 (LAST SEASON); a save from an earlier version ends and a new campaign starts. A new campaign asks how to play: the story (in development, shown but not yet playable) or open-ended operations.
- **Test hook**: `index.html?test` exposes the rules API as `window.SKIRM`, for automated play-throughs. `tools/` holds the soak bots and test pages that use it (see `tools/README.md`).

Operator portraits (90×90) live in `img/operators/` (`m1`–`m10`, `f1`–`f9`, scaled from the sources in `Images/Operators/`). Each operator gets one at recruitment that nobody else alive has; operators with female first names get an `f` portrait, and names anyone might have get either.

`ops.html` only redirects to `index.html`, for old links.
